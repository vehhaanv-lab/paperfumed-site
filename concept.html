The Concept

What could a paper bottle look like?

An exploration of paper-based water packaging. Everything on this page is a research direction, not a validated specification. PaperFumed is an idea, not a product.

Material illustration, not a prototype.

Concept illustration

The idea in one paragraph

The whole point in ten seconds.

PaperFumed is an idea for a drinking bottle whose body is made mainly of paper fibre instead of plastic. It is not a product. There is no prototype, no test data and no company yet. This site is where the idea, the science behind it and the open questions are written down in public.

02

Established evidence

Why a bottle, and why paper

The problem has a number, so the idea does not float.

Companies can produce about 20,000 PET bottles every second, according to the UN Development Programme, and without intervention they persist for roughly 450 years. Globally only about 14% of plastic packaging is collected for recycling and about 40% ends up in landfill, according to the Ellen MacArthur Foundation. Paper fibre comes from a material people already collect and recycle, which is why it is worth asking whether it can hold water.

03

Established evidence

What a paper bottle really is

The hard part, stated before anyone else points it out.

Paper soaks up water, so a bottle for liquids needs a barrier. Paboco describes its current prototype with Coca-Cola as a paper shell with a recyclable plastic lining and cap. Academic reviews name the barrier as the main problem for fibre bottles; a 2023 University of Nottingham review calls barrier functionality “the major issue”. So “paper bottle” in practice usually means paper plus a thin liner, not paper alone.

04

Established evidence

Who we are learning from

The people doing the real work get the credit.

Paboco (The Paper Bottle Company) has worked on paper bottles since 2015 and has piloted with brands including P&G’s Lenor. It says openly which generation of bottle it is on. PaperFumed is not affiliated with Paboco. We study their public work.

06

Unknown

What we are not claiming

Trust is built here.

We do not claim the bottle is compostable, recyclable, plastic-free, safe for drinking, cheaper or better for the climate than plastic. Each of those is a question on the Questions page.

07

Research question

What happens after disposal

End of life is the part most green claims skip.

Three paths exist and each is unproven for this idea: paper recycling, where coatings can interfere; composting, where the liner type decides the outcome; landfill or leakage, where nothing is gained. The Science page covers what the evidence says about each.

Two problems. One material question.

A paper bottle would have to hold water reliably for months, then break down completely afterwards. Whether one structure can do both is still an open question, and it is the question this project exists to answer.
